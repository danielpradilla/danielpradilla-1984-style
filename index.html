1984 Style Guide

A one-bit interface style for Daniel Pradilla's apps, modelled on the 1984 Macintosh: the Finder desktop, MacPaint, the system alerts, and games such as Dark Castle and Battle Chess. Every pixel is black or white.

Link styles.css. It imports pixels.css, the patterns and control bitmaps generated from pixels.txt by ./build.py. Add menubar.js when the app has pull-down menus.

  1. Two values only: --ink and --paper. Grays are dither patterns.
  2. Chicago for menus, titles, buttons and labels; Geneva for content; Monaco for code.
  3. Chicago runs at 16px or 32px. Bitmaps scale by whole numbers only. On screens 1024px and wider, every pixel is drawn 2 CSS px wide.
  4. Selection is inversion: selected items swap black and white.
  5. 1px rules. Windows and menus cast a hard 1px shadow. No blur, no gradients.
  6. One default button per dialog, marked by the heavy ring.
  7. Icons are drawn pixel by pixel in pixels.txt, never traced from photos or scaled down.

Selected state

“Projects” is selected: the icon is inverted inside its mask and the label turns white on black. Draw interiors with white pixels (o) so inversion fills them; transparent pixels (.) stay transparent.

Cursors

--cur-arrow --cur-watch

The desktop uses the arrow everywhere, including links and buttons. Set aria-busy="true" on the desktop, or .busy on a region, to show the watch. Hover over the two samples to try them.
